# Normalization evidence

Generated by `manage.py normalization_evidence`. Nothing here is written by hand, and
nothing was left out because it was unflattering.

## 1. Does the method find the true order?

On real marks nobody knows the true order. So we simulate events in which we do: each
project is given a true quality, each judge a habit (how generous, how wide a scale),
and marks are made from both, with noise, rounded to whole numbers from 1 to 5 on three
criteria. Every method sees the same marks. The code is `judging/evidence.py`.

300 simulated events per panel, 40 projects, 30 judges, the same seeds for every method.

- **Agreement** is Kendall's tau between the method's order and the true order: 1 is the same order, 0 is no relation.
- **Top three found** is the share of the true best three that the method puts in its best three.
- **Winner found** is how often the truly best project is ranked first.
- **Places off** is how many places a project stands from where it belongs, on average.
- **Beats the raw mean** is the share of events in which the method's agreement is higher than the raw mean's.

### What the tables say

- **Even panel**: the ranking score is level with the raw mean (+0.001 in agreement) and better than standard scores per judge (+0.020).
- **Harsh and generous**: the ranking score is better than the raw mean (+0.137 in agreement) and better than standard scores per judge (+0.055).
- **Like the fixture set**: the ranking score is better than the raw mean (+0.075 in agreement) and better than standard scores per judge (+0.029).
- **Few reviews, loud judges**: the ranking score is better than the raw mean (+0.102 in agreement) and better than standard scores per judge (+0.046).
- **No habits at all**: the ranking score is worse than the raw mean (-0.031 in agreement) and better than standard scores per judge (+0.016).

Where judges have habits, correcting for them brings the order closer to the truth, and
the more so the stronger the habits. Where they have none, there is nothing to gain, and
the last table shows what the correction costs in that case. An organizer who is sure
of a panel without habits should rank on the raw mean, which the results page shows
beside the other two. We have not met that panel.

Standard scores per judge are what this portal used until the second day of the event.
These tables are why it no longer does. A judge with three or four reviews has a mean
that says as much about the projects they drew as about them; reading each score
against that mean corrects the judge for the luck of the draw.

### The constant K

A judge's leniency is pulled toward zero as if K reviews at no leniency had been seen
as well. The portal uses K = 1. It was chosen on the panels above, which is the wrong
way round if the tables are read as a test of it; here is what other values give, so
that the choice can be judged. Agreement with the true order, ranking score:

| panel | K = 0.5 | K = 1 | K = 1.5 | K = 2 | K = 3 | K = 6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Even panel | 0.799 | 0.804 | 0.806 | 0.806 | 0.805 | 0.802 |
| Harsh and generous | 0.754 | 0.738 | 0.721 | 0.707 | 0.686 | 0.652 |
| Like the fixture set | 0.781 | 0.778 | 0.773 | 0.768 | 0.759 | 0.741 |
| Few reviews, loud judges | 0.670 | 0.654 | 0.640 | 0.628 | 0.611 | 0.586 |
| No habits at all | 0.807 | 0.814 | 0.819 | 0.821 | 0.824 | 0.828 |

A small K believes the judges' habits sooner, which pays when they are strong and costs
when there are none. K = 1 gives up little in either direction.

## 3. Sample Hack 2026: how far can a place be trusted?

For every project, the best and the worst place it takes when any one judge is taken
out. A place that holds whoever is missing can be trusted. One that swings rests on a
single opinion, and that is nearly always a project with two reviews.

| 8 | Slow Trail | 3 | 3.713 | 6 to 14 |
| 9 | Deep Beacon | 3 | 3.707 | 4 to 16 |
| 10 | Still Beacon | 2 | 3.678 | 7 to 16 |
| 11 | Copper Orbit | 2 | 3.635 | 10 to 13 |
| 12 | Green Switch | 3 | 3.623 | 9 to 25 |

- Over all 40 projects a place moves 13.9 places at most, on average.
- Projects resting on two opinions or fewer: 14.8 places. On four or more: 12.7.

The first twelve places are shown. Where places are close and reviews are few, the
order of neighbours is not settled by the marks alone, under this method or any other.
That is an argument for another review, which is what the organizer's *Reviews per
project* list is for.
